From Idea to $22K/Month: The SuperX SaaS Case Study Explained

Last updated: August 2026

Rob Hallam transformed a solo software project into SuperX, an AI-powered Chrome extension that grew from zero to $22,000 in monthly recurring revenue within 13 months while traveling across fifteen countries, demonstrating that disciplined productivity and strategic platform focus can launch a viable micro-SaaS, according to the Starter Story 2026 case study.

What is SuperX and who is it for?


SuperX is a Chrome extension and content operating system designed for creators and builders on X. Rob Hallam developed the platform to consolidate analytics, growth tracking, and workflow tools into a single browser-based tool. According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, the extension has grown to over 1,400 active Chrome Web Store users. This foundation allows users to manage X growth metrics without leaving their browser.

How much revenue does SuperX generate monthly?


According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, the on-screen badge verified $22,000 in monthly recurring revenue. This figure represents the MRR observed during the investigative session, which the report labels as a verified on-screen badge rather than an independently audited financial total. The data ties this revenue to the platform's $49 monthly subscription tier. Analysts note that such on-screen badges are rare in solo SaaS case studies.

How many active users does the SuperX Chrome extension have?


According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, the SuperX extension shows +1,400 active installs from the Chrome Web Store. The report also connects this user base to 3.7 million indexed social impressions on the X platform, indicating reach among the creator community. These figures are derived from platform telemetry reported by the founder. The extension's presence on the Chrome Web Store serves as a discoverability channel for X-focused tools.

What is the estimated subscriber count for SuperX?


According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, dividing the verified $22,000 monthly revenue by the $49 monthly tier price yields an estimated ~449 paying subscribers, as calculated within the report. The report explicitly flags this as a calculated estimate, not a direct count from billing systems. Such calculated estimates offer practical insight into the user-to-revenue ratio for the extension. Such calculated estimates are common in investigative case studies where full financial disclosure is not always available.

How did relocating to Colombia impact SuperX's growth?


According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, after 12 months of zero income and fragmented travel, Rob Hallam settled in Colombia for three consecutive months starting in July 2025, a period he cites as the key pivot for breaking through the revenue plateau. The case study reports that this residency enabled deeper focus and consistent development velocity. The data notes this as a founder-reported milestone rather than a universal formula. Relocation to a single region is one of many strategies documented in solo SaaS journeys.

What is Rob Hallam's productivity philosophy?


According to the Starter Story 2026 case study, the founder-reported routine includes 8 hours in a nocturnal deep-flow state block and 9 hours of sleep per night, across 13+ countries traveled. Hallam's core philosophy frames productivity as downstream of emotional well-being, labeling the principle "Productivity Follows Happiness." The data presents this as a reported correlation, not a universal rule applicable to all developers. Individual productivity patterns vary widely, and the case study frames this as one founder's observed trend.
